# Explainer styles and references

Cutline includes four starting styles for explaining an idea, product or process. Each changes the scene layouts and visual treatment. All remain editable after assembly. Start with the [explainer guide](explainer-guide.md) to make a video.

## Choose a style

| Style                 | Good for                                             | What Cutline builds                                                                                                    | What you supply                                                                         |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Kinetic type**      | A concise concept, announcement or lesson            | Large type, controlled scale and entrance motion, and a repeating geometric motif                                      | A written script and optional brand colors                                              |
| **Product focus**     | A product task or feature walkthrough                | An opening, framed screen or split scenes, and a closing action; short scripts may use screen scenes throughout        | A factual script and real screenshots or recordings for every screen scene              |
| **Sketch flow**       | Steps, relationships or a simple comparison          | Editable process nodes with drawn connectors and two-sided comparisons; diagrams stack on portrait and square canvases | Correct steps and relationships in your script, with labels refined in the scene editor |
| **Editorial collage** | An editorial explanation or a more tactile statement | Layered paper-colored shapes, restrained rotation, native typography, statement scenes and editable cutout labels      | A written script; no photos are required for this starter                               |

The Editorial collage starter is a paper-and-type composition. It does not automatically find photographs, remove backgrounds or generate documentary imagery. Import owned or licensed media separately when your story needs it. Product focus requires real visual evidence; the other three can be created using local text and graphics alone.

For a person speaking to camera, use the existing [Creator punch or Screen demo](creator-shorts.md) formats instead. Those preserve the speaker's recorded voice while showing source-timed proof shots.

## Reference notes

Research reviewed October 23, 2026. These directions draw on current product releases, established motion-design practice and a creative trend report. They are recommendations for useful explainer structures, not a ranking of viral formats. Popularity and conversion performance were not measured.

The research used publisher descriptions and release materials rather than frame-by-frame video analysis. The Cutline implementations are original starting treatments. Reference footage, brand assets and voices are not included.

### Product focus

Linear's [Introducing Linear Agent](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-24-introducing-linear-agent), published March 24, 2026, and [Introducing Loops](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-07-20-introducing-loops), published July 20, 2026, provide recent examples of explaining a product through actual tasks and visible workflow behavior. Loops describes triggers and execution history.

The Cutline recommendation is to establish the task, show a legible action and hold on the real result. Screen framing and pacing are our proposed treatment, not measured reproductions of those films. A screenshot should support the spoken claim; a decorative interface is not proof that a workflow happened.

### Kinetic type

Figma's [Principles in motion](https://www.figma.com/blog/principles-in-motion/), published June 24, 2026, discusses sequencing ideas, meaningful timing and motion's relationship to sound. Its [Config 2025 visual identity](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-we-shaped-the-visual-identity-for-config-2025/), published July 3, 2025, describes expressive glyphs built from basic forms.

Cutline applies those broad principles through short statements, a clear type hierarchy and a recurring shape. Keep one idea dominant at a time and leave enough time to read it. This is current craft evidence for a recurring approach, not evidence that animated type alone makes a video perform well.

### Sketch flow

Google's [Video Overviews announcement](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/07/video-overviews-studio-panel-updates-notebooklm.html), published July 30, 2025, describes narrated explanations using diagrams, images, quotations and numbers from supplied material. The [official introduction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KA_pExdDSUo) accompanies that product direction.

Cutline's Sketch flow starter makes steps and simple comparisons editable. Use the diagram to show what enters, what changes and what comes out. Verify the relationships yourself: arranging words into nodes does not establish that a process or causal claim is correct.

### Editorial collage

BUCK's [Asana Brand Film](https://buck.co/work/asana-brand-film) describes bringing product UI and human storytelling together through typography, collage and handmade visual elements. Its publication date was not established, so it is a craft precedent rather than a claimed new release. Adobe's [2026 Creative Trends report](https://business.adobe.com/resources/creative-trends-report.html) supports sensory texture and human connection as broader creative themes.

Cutline's initial treatment uses native paper shapes and type. Keep overlaps and rotation restrained, preserve readable contrast and use real source material when adding imagery. The trend report supports a direction, not a promise about this preset's reach or engagement.

## Make the style serve the explanation

Write the complete narration first, then shorten on-screen copy. Keep speech captions separate from headings. Use short labels for process nodes and comparison sides, and inspect dense scenes at the intended viewing size.

Durations follow the script and actual voiceover rather than a fixed template runtime. Review the opening, the busiest scene, transitions, the final spoken words and the exported audio. Native layouts support editable 2D motion. They do not promise arbitrary path morphing, true 3D, face tracking, automated character animation or exact replication of another creator's work.
