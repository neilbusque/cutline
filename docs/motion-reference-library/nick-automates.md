## Direct visual observations

1. **Proof above a person, then proof takes over.** In `a99VVqGgVM8` 0.0-3.2s, a pale textured vertical background holds a small terminal/demo in the upper part and a real speaking subject in the lower half. The top clip actually changes from terminal to finished typography while the speaker gestures. At ~3.6s, the composition cuts to a larger editor/timeline view against a dark background. The screen demonstration becomes the subject. At 11.0-11.4s the speaker is a tight full-frame crop, followed by a white enumeration scene at 11.8-13.4s. These are meaningful format changes, not successive copies of one layout.
2. **Type has roles.** The hook uses black sans-serif support plus blue rectangular emphasis and a contrasting italic serif on important words. Live captions are small phrases that update as speech develops; some words are blue/italic rather than every word being boxed. The enumeration scene separates ordinal, actual product screenshot, and product name. Copying this exact blue/serif palette into every film would recreate the repetition problem.
3. **Screens are evidence, not wallpaper.** In `a99VVqGgVM8` 9.0-10.6s the upper editor demonstrates audio clips and timing while the narration/captions refer to timeline placement/level. In `pqDxElXLZE4`, the sheet shows prompt input, generated output, audience questions, a node graph, and sharing. Each view has a different instructional job.
4. **Inversion marks chapters.** The recent sound-effects and prompt-library Shorts switch between white/pale speaker compositions and dark proof compositions. White enumeration cards are temporary punctuation. `J33PnS6vyiA` uses lime accents and black backgrounds for the tool's visual identity, rather than the blue from the other two examples.
5. **Motion is often nested.** `bHIMNW_4mlg` 0.4-2.8s contains an animated title demonstration within a browser inset while the speaker remains active. At 3.2s, the frame cuts to the original social post, then progressively shows gallery examples. The meaningful object changes; the edit is not just a background zoom.
6. **Payoff before CTA.** Opal's sheet shows a working graph and share screen before returning to the presenter. The Katana sheet shows finished generated footage shortly before the final presenter CTA. A faceless adaptation needs its own visible result in this slot.
7. **Color is relational.** The speaking subject retains natural skin and darker clothing on a pale background; product screens keep source whites/blacks. Dark proof scenes isolate the content. These samples do not prove LUT names, white-balance settings, or filter values.

## Source-stated sound workflow, not independently verified product claims

The creator's description and auto captions for `a99VVqGgVM8` name **Openverse API**, **Real SFX**, and **Earshot**. The stated division is: retrieve sounds, spot them against visual events, synthesize a missing sound when needed. This strongly explains Neil's Openverse request. Verify each product's official documentation and actual licensing independently; the video's number of available sounds and statements about free use are not implementation requirements.

The video points to **Prompt Motion** as a library of motion examples with prompts. The frame at ~3.6s shows `prompt-motion.com`. The spoken/visual count and written-description count differ, so do not treat a count as verified. The useful pattern is example + result + prompt + user customization, not blind reuse of an entire finished look.

## Recommended Cutline translation (authored recommendations)

- Add a shot role to every beat: promise, action, proof, explanation, payoff, CTA. Require an asset or native action that performs the role.
- Support multiple composition families: presenter/proof split; full-screen product detail; functional diagram; kinetic statement; before/after comparison; process footage; clean end frame. Select by story and assets. A presenter slot requires real presenter media.
- Use scene-level fingerprints: dominant subject, composition family, scale, background value, movement, transition. Reject runs of identical fingerprints and repeated project-wide opening/closing signatures. Merely swapping the accent color does not count as variation.
- Use full-frame product detail at least once when the story depends on software interaction. Let screens occupy enough pixels to read at phone size.
- Add semantic sound spotting: onset frame, action, intensity, duration, speech overlap, source/license, optional fade. Use silence and holds between important accents; do not add a whoosh to every cut.
- Separate correction from creative grade: normalize exposure/white balance first; match skin and screen whites; choose a deliberate scene look second; preserve readable screen content.
- Make reference records searchable by technique and evidence level. Keep creator identity and palette as a reference field, not a global preset.
