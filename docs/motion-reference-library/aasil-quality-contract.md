# Aasil quality contract

Aasil is the primary creative reference for this workflow. The target is a directed film with confident type, tangible depth, purposeful camera movement, deliberate color, and an outcome that the viewer actually sees. Read the specific source passage before selecting a treatment. The channel contains tutorials, client work, presenter Shorts, and filmed process footage, so its identity cannot be reduced to one palette or preset.

This contract separates **observed source behavior** from **Cutline starting values and acceptance criteria**. The numbers below are authored production choices, not measurements of Aasil's project files, lenses, LUTs, easing curves, or sound mix. Native presets are editable starting material. Stacking three presets is not, by itself, a finished film.

## Choose the actual film treatment

Choose a primary treatment from the story and available assets. A meaningful change of treatment may mark a narrative chapter, but a film should still share one clear design system.

| Treatment         | Material and hierarchy                                                                                                                | Motion and depth                                                                                                                         | Color decision                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Kinetic proof** | A large real interface, chart, object, or diagram is the subject. Short display type introduces or resolves its action.               | Input becomes output, a path resolves, a comparison completes, or a detail enlarges. Type and marks react to the same event.             | Neutral screen whites/blacks with a deliberate brand accent. Invert the background at a chapter only when it improves separation. |
| **Depth launch**  | One hero screen or object leads, supported by smaller contextual planes and expressive title type.                                    | Establish perspective, move through the layers, then settle into a readable proof view. A shared object or axis connects the next scene. | Choose the product's palette and material. Dark, pale, saturated, or warm scenes are valid when coherent with the product.        |
| **Maker story**   | Real presenter/process footage carries the story, with tool screens, a clock, a client artifact, and the finished result as evidence. | Cut between real subject scales; let a gesture or action motivate a punch-in. Graphic accents explain specific points.                   | Correct each source first, then match warmth and contrast while protecting skin and screen content.                               |

A presenter treatment requires suitable presenter footage. With no presenter, author a faceless proof or launch treatment and identify the adaptation. Repeated cards cannot substitute for a speaking person's gesture, expression, or delivery.

## Composition, typography, and depth

Use the following as starting choices, then inspect the actual frame at phone size.

- Give each beat one dominant subject and a clear visual verb: travel, reveal, align, compare, select, enlarge, resolve. A heading and unrelated stock image do not perform the verb.
- For a 1080x1920 film, start a principal display line around 90-144px, supporting type around 38-64px, and captions around 44-64px. Fit the actual phrase, line count, and font; these ranges are not pass/fail substitutes for reading the frame. A caption and a product-screen label need different scales.
- Use a deliberate type contrast, such as heavy display sans with a sparse italic serif accent. Keep the font system to one or two families. If the exact condensed type is unavailable, choose an available licensed face and validate its actual shape rather than calling an ordinary font an exact match.
- Reserve a calm caption band and platform-safe region. Do not cover a face, a hand that performs the action, a cursor, an input field, or the final result. Keep decorative rules and underlines out of headlines.
- In a depth scene, separate foreground, hero plane, and background by scale, overlap, parallax, focus, and lighting. Perspective should reveal their relationship. Start a screen tilt around 8-22 degrees and settle toward 0-6 degrees for the reading hold, when appropriate. These are proposed starting values, not measured source values.
- Preserve screen geometry. A UI plane may rotate during travel, but a view that carries essential text should become sufficiently frontal and large to read. If that requires a full-screen detail, cut or travel into one.
- Distinguish a 2.5D screen plane from true 3D. Plane perspective and layered parallax are useful for Aasil-style screen graphics. They do not establish mesh modeling, a real 3D camera, volumetric lights, physical reflections, or depth-of-field. Do not claim those capabilities from a tilted rectangle.
- Let one action lead. A word, pointer, screen plane, and decorative background all moving with equal strength destroy hierarchy. Ambient movement is optional and should not compete with the proof.

## Pacing and continuity

Write a compact beat sheet before generating layers: promise, action, development, proof, visible payoff, close. Follow actual narration when it exists.

- Start with a specific subject and promise in roughly the first two seconds when the format calls for a fast hook. Do not prepend a generic logo animation by habit.
- Alternate short impact, developing action, and readable hold. Start important entrances around 8-18 frames at 30fps and key outcome holds around 0.8-1.6 seconds, then adjust to the actual reading load and voice. A source operation or explanation may need much longer.
- Cut when an idea, gesture, action, or meaningful audio event justifies it. An arbitrary cut every two seconds can be as repetitive as no cuts.
- Bridge scenes with a shared object, axis, color mass, screen detail, or action where useful. A continuous cursor, packet, letter, or camera move can make the next scene feel inevitable. Hard cuts are valid when they create a useful change of scale or thought.
- Compare adjacent beats by subject, material, scale, composition, motion, and transition. Four consecutive generic title/card beats need a new visual idea. Changing only their background colors is insufficient. Keep a continuing demonstration when its state genuinely develops.
- Keep one continuous narration source. Proof clips should not accidentally add duplicate voice or unrelated recorded sound. A source passage must continue through `sourceIn` when the action continues; restarting a clip must be intentional.

## Color and effects

Correct, match, and then style. Color grading is part of directing the material, not a filter-strength lottery.

1. Inspect each source's exposure, white balance, clipping, and skin before applying a look. Preserve source neutrals where the viewer needs to read an interface.
2. Match adjacent sources by their actual appearance. Identical slider values do not establish a match.
3. Choose a shared grade and strength, then make shot-specific corrections. `studio`, `soft-film`, and `crisp-mono` are starting looks. They do not perform automatic skin, exposure, or camera matching.
4. Use a saturated accent, selective glow, blur, grain, light leak, or RGB separation only when it has a film-specific purpose. Do not apply every effect or make every scene dark to imply quality. A light screen and a dark cinematic scene may belong together when their transition is deliberate.
5. Review entrances, settled frames, cuts, gradients, skin, and text after export. Check dark proof scenes for lost details and pale scenes for clipped screen whites.

Warm presenter lighting, strong blue/lavender graphic examples, neutral UI, client-specific pink/magenta, and black/olive-lit perspective screen graphics are visible in the inspected sources. The masterclass uses selective bloom/light around the plane, while the client goals sequence shares a saturated magenta field. These are film materials, not app-shell design rules. The sources do not establish a universal Aasil LUT or a compulsory purple palette.

## Sound contract and limits

Spot sound against visual actions: onset frame, action, texture, intensity, length, speech overlap, fade, and source license. Openverse can supply licensed material; the library does not decide whether a sound belongs in the scene. Use fewer meaningful accents, preserve deliberate silence, and keep dialogue intelligible.

Actual playback with sound is required to judge sync, pacing, balance, or musical payoff. A spectrogram, waveform, subtitle file, still contact sheet, or passing decode cannot establish those subjective qualities. Do not report them as reviewed until they were heard.

## Practical acceptance gates

| Gate          | Required evidence before claiming the film is ready                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Story**     | The opening promise leads to an action and a result that is visibly shown. Copy announcing a result is insufficient.                                                                                                          |
| **Variation** | A 20-40 second finished film normally contains at least three purposeful composition mechanisms. A continuous demonstration can be exempt when it meaningfully develops. No unexplained run of four generic card/title beats. |
| **Hierarchy** | At 390px viewing width, the principal subject, required words, and relevant control/result are readable without pausing to zoom. Inspect the actual frame, not only numeric font size.                                        |
| **Depth**     | Perspective preserves a coherent plane and keeps critical information readable at its hold. No unintended clipping, flipped plane, edge-on disappearance, or inconsistent text/shape tilt.                                    |
| **Motion**    | Inspect first, developing, settled, and exit moments; both sides of each significant cut; and a moving 5-8 second representative passage before a long remake. Fix the reference comparison before rendering the full film.   |
| **Color**     | Adjacent shots match intentionally, skin is credible, screen neutrals remain readable, and the creative grade supports the chosen material. Synthetic blocks are technical QA only.                                           |
| **Audio**     | One intended voice path, controlled proof sound, attributed source assets, and complete listening review. Record an explicit limitation if listening was unavailable.                                                         |
| **Delivery**  | Editable native project saves and reopens with its media; MP4 dimensions/duration/audio are probed and fully decoded; full playback and the subjective review boundary are recorded separately.                               |

Record the exact source URL and inspected passage, the chosen treatment, the authored values, the rendered comparison, and remaining limitations in the film's brief. Creator-inspired style names are a way to find evidence, not a claim of matching quality.
