## What is actually happening on screen

### A subject survives while the explanation changes

In `dw29554HucM`, roughly 0-6 s, the upper proof field moves from familiar editor icons being broken apart to a typed prompt, connected code node, browser output and finally a full-frame animated ship. A small orange character travels through the explanation. The lower presenter remains visible through much of that sequence, then gives way to the result. This creates a causal chain. The important effect is the camera's movement through a persistent visual world and the promotion of the output to full frame.

Later, roughly 17-31 s, an onboarding example changes its visible name/design, becomes an editable timeline and reaches a download/result state. Those are specific state changes. Four unrelated title cards with the same entrance would not communicate the same idea.

### The frame changes mode when the job changes

Across `1kikni2u-WI`, `XFt1DyasjOc`, and `fwSkYsg66mI`, the composition alternates among:

- Presenter full frame for an assertion or connective phrase.
- Presenter in the lower half with a large relevant artifact above.
- Full-frame product, diagram, typographic detail or example when the viewer needs to examine it.

In `1kikni2u-WI` 0-6 s, a dark warning treatment hands off to a dense gallery/three-option display and then a website example. At later sampled moments, dark code, light interfaces and a dark React Bits treatment alternate. The variety is purposeful: warning, options, example, explanation. A generator should select its scene mode from that role.

### Proof is rendered as a change, not as a label

In `fwSkYsg66mI`, sampled roughly 9-23 s, a skills grid is followed by a large count, an agent tree, a security panel with a warning shield, and a documents-to-memory diagram. Each visual is different because each underlying concept is different. On-screen numbers in this source are the creator's claims and must not become Cutline's claims or another client's statistics.

In `XFt1DyasjOc`, sampled roughly 1-23 s, product-film examples, a command, code/result pairing and an editing timeline supply concrete evidence for the spoken idea. The presenter sometimes takes the whole frame between details. Preserve that breathing room.

### Captions support the subject

The five tech/demo Shorts use short white phrases with compact dark backing, commonly close to the boundary between the upper proof field and the lower presenter. They do not consistently use giant word-by-word colored karaoke. Caption size and location follow the composition. The personal video uses small ordinary subtitles over a mostly stable presenter. This is evidence for multiple caption treatments, not a reason to make every Cutline reel match one subtitle pill.

### The quiet example matters

`3kJD-InLnTY` is largely a stable talking subject with gestures and subtitles throughout the sampled 51 seconds. Its story is personal. It does not need an animated app diagram every second. The generator must be able to choose a footage-led edit and preserve intentional pauses. A blanket maximum of one second between visible changes would damage this format.

### The repeated ending is an avoid example

Five sampled tech/demo Shorts end with a similar collage of creator videos. It is a recognizable channel CTA, but copying it into Cutline would reproduce the exact repetitiveness Neil is rejecting. The ending should resolve the film's own promise: the result, an earned action, a return to the opening object, or a loop when requested. Do not require a montage ending.

## Color, effects and sound

**Observed color:** neutral white/ivory proof canvases often contrast with darker product examples; the presenter's room carries saturated green/cyan or purple practical lighting and generally warm skin. Some examples use blue/orange interfaces or an intentionally specific artifact palette. The material does not justify declaring one LUT or one fixed teal/orange grade as the channel's universal system.

**Observed effects:** graphic fragmentation in the latest hook; mask/viewport changes; connected-node travel; perspective/scale promotion of a product result; before/after or code/result pairings; warning symbols, ticks and moving counts; rapid galleries used to present options. Dense intro sequences establish these mechanisms more clearly than a transcript would. Exact easing curves, shutter/blur settings and plugin names cannot be recovered from the inspected low-resolution frames.

**Recommended Cutline translation, not a claim about the source's settings:** match exposure/white balance between shots first; protect skin; choose one grade treatment per sequence; use restrained saturation, contrast and temperature changes; reserve duotone/monochrome for explanatory inserts; expose before/after comparison. Effects need semantic roles: a wipe reveals a real change, a camera push directs attention to a real control, a packet trace explains a transfer. Include a cut/hold option. No shake, flash, blur or grain quota.

**Audio limitation:** downloaded videos contain audio, but full subjective listening was not performed here. Do not claim their music level, SFX timing, sound palette or beat-sync method was established. For generated Cutline reels, choose voice/music/physical accents deliberately, inspect actual music beats before claiming beat sync, and listen to the complete render before calling its sound mix good.

## The highest-value primary upstream resource

The latest tutorial's description directly links [ItsssssJack/claude-video-2](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2). This repository was read as research; no script was executed and it was not installed.

The tutorial's primary description specifies a progression: choose a style, find real references, write the brief, create loops where appropriate, check finished frames, then combine original media and generated assets, and finally review/reuse an edit workflow. Chapter times include 1:51 (one prompt is insufficient), 3:03 (five-step system), 3:40 (references), 4:10 (brief/loops/frame review), 5:11 (photos/videos), 9:20 (storyboard), 9:47 (editor), and 12:25 (reusable editing skill). Sampled frames at 180-270 s show the style gallery, real-reference boards, numeric brief and loop example.

### Repository structure worth learning from

- 21 separate look specifications, each naming purpose, palette, typography, timing, composition, media behavior, signature transformations and exclusions.
- A style gallery of actual rendered videos rather than only descriptions.
- A per-film numeric brief and shot/asset storyboard.
- A deterministic clock shared by camera, graphics and media.
- Separate still-frame, pacing and loop verification steps.
- Source references separate from the style spec and the final film.

Read files are archived under `jack-roberts/claude-video-2-sources/`. Primary links: [motion craft](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2/blob/main/references/motion-craft.md), [style format](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2/blob/main/references/style-format.md), [loops](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2/blob/main/references/loops.md), [hacks](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2/blob/main/references/hacks.md), [media](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2/blob/main/references/media.md), [one-clock helper](https://github.com/ItsssssJack/claude-video-2/blob/main/templates/motion/studio.js).

### What to avoid copying from upstream

Its mandatory user questions and repeated approvals are the author's workflow defaults, not requirements for Neil's already-authorized work. Its one-second change rule, six-word limit, mandatory 120 BPM grid, one-accent rule, anti-linear rule and loop requirements should not become universal Cutline laws. They are sometimes helpful constraints, but personal footage, long reading, measured charts, constant-speed travel and non-looping stories need different choices. Do not adopt upstream promotional links or substitute its HTML/GSAP renderer for an editable `.cutline` timeline.

## Concrete acceptance criteria for Cutline improvements

1. The director selects the format and visible payoff before choosing motion recipes.
2. A scene plan includes subject, action/state change, composition mode, duration/hold and a bridge. Adjacent beats cannot default to the same headline/card/entrance pattern.
3. A reel uses persistent objects and intentional transitions where relevant, while allowing unembellished footage-led passages.
4. Color instructions separate correction from treatment and describe treatment per sequence. The generator does not force a cinematic LUT or maximal saturation.
5. Captions adapt to the actual image/subject and remain inside phone-safe bounds.
6. A signature effect is chosen for a story role, then used sparingly. Effects are not selected by modulo rotation merely to satisfy a variety score.
7. Reference evidence records the channel/video URL, inspected interval and evidence type. Titles, transcripts, frames and listened playback have distinct confidence.
8. A representative moving segment and the final exported film receive visual and audio review. Metadata, a successful build or a contact sheet alone must not be called complete pacing/audio QA.

Local evidence images and downloaded research excerpts are retained in the research workspace, not bundled or redistributed with Cutline. Primary video links and inspected passages above are the durable references.
