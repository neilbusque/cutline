# Sound spotting

Sound follows the action and story. Plan voice, music, physical accents and silence before filling every cut with effects. Keep them on independent editable tracks.

| Frame/time          | Visible event                    | Sound role                      | Length/level                        | Source and license                     | Check                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Actual chosen frame | Packet arrives or result appears | Small restrained arrival accent | Trim to event; below speech         | Local asset or returned Openverse UUID | Hear it against the voice               |
| Actual chosen frame | Real click or material action    | Corresponding physical sound    | Match actual action                 | Retain creator/source/license          | Avoid false action or distracting level |
| Actual chosen range | Important line resolves          | Silence or music release        | Leave last syllable and tail intact | Existing music, if any                 | Full playback                           |

Use [Openverse](../openverse.md) for key-free audio and image search. Search a concrete audible event, preview candidate sounds, import the one that fits, and retain source credits. Openverse indexes licenses; the source license still governs attribution and share-alike obligations. Freesound previews can be lower quality than original WAV files.

Adding audio places an audible clip with a starting level and short fades. It does not spot the sound, normalize loudness, sync it to a beat or make the mix good automatically. Move and trim it against the actual visual event, then listen at normal volume with speech.

Start a music bed below speech and adjust by listening and measurement. `audio.duck` is available for linked music/speech clips. Analyze actual music before claiming beat alignment. Let physical accents and silence carry meaning; repeating the same whoosh is another form of repetitive editing.

These channel studies used frame samples and available captions/descriptions. They support no claim about the creators' exact mix levels or SFX timing. A complete render needs continuous audiovisual review before its pacing, pronunciation, caption sync or mix can be certified. Decode, loudness and true-peak checks are useful technical evidence and cannot replace listening.
